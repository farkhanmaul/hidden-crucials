## 🧩 **Mental Models Reference Library**

### 🏛️ **Core Mental Models** (Must Know)
1. **Inversion** - Think backwards from desired outcome
2. **Circle of Competence** - Know what you know and don't know
3. **Opportunity Cost** - Everything has a trade-off
4. **Compound Interest** - Small consistent actions = big results
5. **Pareto Principle** - 80/20 rule for prioritization

### ⚖️ **Decision Models** (For Choices)
1. **Expected Value** - Probability × Impact calculation
2. **Sunk Cost** - Don't chase good money with bad
3. **Satisficing** - Good enough vs perfect optimization
4. **Optionality** - Keep multiple paths open
5. **Reversible vs Irreversible** - Different decision frameworks

### 🔄 **Systems Models** (For Complexity)
1. **Feedback Loops** - Reinforcing vs balancing cycles
2. **Leverage Points** - Where to intervene in a system
3. **Network Effects** - Value increases with adoption
4. **Emergence** - System properties > sum of parts
5. **Lindy Effect** - Time-tested ideas survive longer

### 🧪 **Learning Models** (For Growth)
1. **Scientific Method** - Hypothesis → Test → Update
2. **Falsifiability** - What would prove this wrong?
3. **Survivorship Bias** - Don't just study the winners
4. **Regression to the Mean** - Extreme results tend to normalize
5. **Antifragility** - Systems that get stronger from stress

## 💡 **Article Writing Framework**

### 📋 **Standard Structure**
1. **Hook** - Compelling problem or question
2. **Framework Introduction** - What it is, why it matters
3. **Deep Dive** - How it works, examples, applications
4. **Implementation** - Practical steps to use it
5. **Advanced Applications** - Complex scenarios
6. **Common Pitfalls** - What to avoid
7. **Action Steps** - What to do next

### 🔍 **Research Sources**
- Academic papers in cognitive science
- Business case studies
- Historical examples
- Personal experiments and applications
- Cross-disciplinary insights

### 🎯 **Learning Objectives**
Each article should help readers:
- Understand a specific mental model or framework
- Recognize when and how to apply it
- Avoid common mistakes in application
- Connect it to other thinking tools
- Practice with real-world examples
